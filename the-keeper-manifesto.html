The Keeper Manifesto

A three-minute read

Every band has one.

The one who books the room and confirms it twice. The one who builds the setlist and prints five copies because somebody always forgets theirs. The one who knows the sub’s phone number, the venue’s load-in door, the key the singer actually sings it in. The one who texts “we still on for Tuesday?” and gets two thumbs-ups, one “should be,” and silence.

Nobody elected you. There was no meeting. You just noticed, somewhere along the way, that if you didn’t do it, it didn’t happen — and the band mattered too much to let it not happen.

So you carry it.

Here’s what nobody says out loud: the band doesn’t know how much you carry, because carrying it well makes it invisible. The rehearsal that starts on time looks like luck. The gig that runs smooth looks like everyone’s professionalism. The book that’s always current looks like it updates itself. Your best work is indistinguishable from nothing having happened at all.

And once in a while — be honest — you resent it. Then you feel bad for resenting it, because nobody asked you to do it. Then Tuesday comes, and the count-off lands, and for three and a half minutes the whole invisible spreadsheet in your head goes quiet, and you remember exactly why you do this.

This is for you.

Not another tool to manage — you have enough to manage. A place to put some of it down. The decisions, kept. The parts, known. The plan, seen by everyone without you texting twice. The memory of the band living somewhere besides your head — so that when you walk into the room, you get to be what you actually joined as:

a musician.

The band will probably never fully know what you carried. But we do.

Protect the Keeper.
