An honest comparison

GrooveLinx vs. your group chat + a Drive folder

Let’s start with the truth: the stack you already have is free, familiar, and everyone in the band knows how to use it. We ran our own band on it for years — group chat, a Drive folder, voice memos, and hope. If it’s genuinely working for you, keep it. We mean that.

But if you’re here, some part of it probably isn’t working. Here’s the part.

The stack stores files. It doesn’t keep the thread.

Nothing in it knows what your band decided, what’s ready, or what Tuesday needs. The chat holds every message, which is exactly why you can’t find the one that matters. The folder holds every file, which is exactly why nobody knows which chart is current. The knowledge your band actually runs on — the ending you fixed, the key you changed, who starts the song, which twelve of your hundred songs are gig-ready tonight — lives in nobody’s folder. It lives in somebody’s head. Usually the same somebody.

That’s the difference, and it’s the whole difference. GrooveLinx isn’t a better folder. It’s the thing the folder never was: your band’s memory. Decisions kept where you’ll find them at the moment you need them. One current version of every song, every chart, every plan. And the person who’s been holding it all in their head gets to put some of it down.

The stack costs nothing and forgets everything. That trade is fine — until it isn’t.

Founding bands can bring their whole book over — paste a setlist, and it takes minutes, not weekends. Bring your band.

The software disappears. The music remains.
