The Room

How to Run a Rehearsal People Want to Come Back To

A four-minute read

Every rehearsal competes with a couch.

Not with another band, not with another hobby — with a warm living room, a person your bandmate loves, and a show they’re halfway through. That’s the real opponent, and it makes its offer every single week. Bands don’t lose members to drama nearly as often as they lose them to Tuesday nights that stopped being worth the drive.

So the question isn’t “how do I make my band more disciplined.” It’s: what makes a person get in the car in the rain, again, in year three?

Start with music inside ten minutes. Not talk about the gig, not the parking situation, not the PA hum. The longer the front porch of a rehearsal, the more it feels like a meeting with instruments. Handle logistics some other way, any other way. People came to play.

Have one intention, said out loud. Not an agenda — an intention. “Tonight the second set stops being scary.” One sentence. It costs nothing and it changes the whole room, because now the night can be won. A rehearsal that can’t be won can only be endured.

People come back to rooms where something happened. Make sure something can happen.

End on something that works. Never end on the trainwreck. Run the trainwreck at 8:40, not 9:55. The last fifteen minutes are for a song the band loves and owns, because the feeling everyone drives home with is the memory of the night, and that memory is what gets weighed against the couch next week. This is not sentimental — it’s just how memory works, and bands are made of memory.

Let people be heard about the music. The fastest way to kill a room is for the same two people to decide everything while three others quietly conclude their opinion is decorative. You don’t need consensus votes. You need the drummer’s idea about the ending to get tried once, tonight, for real — even if it dies. Ideas that get tried keep coming. Ideas that get nodded at and skipped stop.

And don’t restart every week. This is the quiet killer. The ending you fixed two weeks ago gets re-argued because nobody remembers what was decided. Twenty minutes gone, and worse than the minutes — the feeling that the work doesn’t hold. Nobody drives through rain to re-do finished work. Keep what the band decides, however you keep it, so Tuesday starts from where Tuesday actually is.

Nobody quits a band that’s getting somewhere. People quit bands that are treading water with a good drummer.

None of this needs a whiteboard or a band cop. It’s five habits and one sentence at the top of the night. The couch is patient and it never has an off week. The room has to be worth it on purpose.

Asked and answered

How do you keep band members motivated to come to rehearsal?

Make rehearsals winnable: music in ten minutes, one spoken intention, and end on something that works. Momentum beats obligation.

How long should a band rehearsal be?

Length matters less than shape. A focused two hours that ends on a win beats a meandering four that ends on the trainwreck.

Why do band members stop showing up?

Usually not drama. Rehearsals stopped producing progress that holds week to week, so the couch’s standing offer started winning.

The software disappears. The music remains.
